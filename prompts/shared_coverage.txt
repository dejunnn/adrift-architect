## Coverage

Before writing any $artefact, decompose the ADR into clauses for the individual constraints its decision states and account for every one of them.
Quote each clause, explain why it is or is not enforceable, and then give the verdict:

COVERED: what you return below enforces the whole clause. List the ids in checkIds. gap is "".
PARTIAL: they enforce some of it. List them, and put in gap exactly what is not checked.
UNSUPPORTED: it cannot be checked automatically. checkIds is empty. Say why in reason.

$field records what you evaluated for this clause, not just what you used.
Recording an option you then rejected is more informative than omitting it. It shows the choice was a decision rather than an oversight, and reason should explain why you rejected it. $options

gap is required. A clause marked PARTIAL with an empty gap is a claim that nothing is missing, which contradicts PARTIAL.
A clause marked COVERED with a non-empty gap is really PARTIAL.

checkIds is a list because one clause might need more than one $artefact.
A decision about both source shape and compiled dependencies takes two, and neither alone is COVERED.
List every one that contributes. The same id may appear under more than one clause.

Account for every clause, including the ones that are not enforceable.
An ADR with nothing enforceable in it is a real and common answer, but it has to be stated clause by clause.
A reply with no clauses is indistinguishable from one where decomposition was never attempted.
Most decision records contain clauses that no tool can check.
Marking it UNSUPPORTED with a reason is the correct answer, not a failure.

Every id in checkIds must be one you actually return.
